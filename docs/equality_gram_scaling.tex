\begin{abstract}
Equality whitening replaces a scalar dual gradient step by an equality Gram
solve without changing the model or the HPR splitting. The coordinate change
must include the dual variables, the row proximal metric, and the restart and
penalty geometry. We derive the conservative mixed-row metric used by the
implementation, distinguish its safeguarded update from an undamped ADMM
subproblem, and state eligibility, fallback, and independent polishing contracts.
Algebraic correctness does not imply a runtime improvement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, signs, and coordinates}
Consider the convex native problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 \min_{x\in B}\;\tfrac12 x^\top Qx+c^\top x+c_0,
 \qquad Ex=b,\qquad Cx-d\in D.
\end{equation}
Here $Q\succeq0$, $B$ is a box, and $D$ is a Cartesian product of interval and
Lorentz-cone blocks. ``Native'' means the QP or lifted conic model supplied to
the solver; it does not mean a separate original-QCQP stopping test. Rows can
be gathered into this partition without changing their stored order. Assume
$E\in\R^{e\times n}$ has full row rank and define
\[
 A=\begin{bmatrix}E\\C\end{bmatrix},\quad
 G=EE^\top\succ0,\quad G=LL^\top,\quad
 \beta>0,\quad\beta\ge\norm{C}_2^2.
\]
The implementation bounds $\norm{C}_2^2$ by
$\norm{C}_1\norm{C}_\infty$, with a rounding margin; it uses $\beta=1$ if the
other-row operator is zero. Sparse factorization applies $G^{-1}$ without
forming a dense inverse or whitening matrix.

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W=\operatorname{diag}(L^{-1},\beta^{-1/2}I),\qquad
 \widetilde A=WA,\qquad \widetilde y=W^{-\top}y.
\end{equation}
The equality becomes $L^{-1}Ex=L^{-1}b$. The other rows, their affine offsets,
and finite interval endpoints are scaled by $\beta^{-1/2}$; a Lorentz cone is
unchanged as a set by this positive scalar scaling. In particular,
\[
 \widetilde A^\top\widetilde y=A^\top y,\qquad
 \ip{\widetilde y}{Wv}=\ip{y}{v}.
\]
Thus stationarity and the primal--dual pairing are preserved. Dense row mixing
is confined to equalities. Applying $L^{-1}$ to arbitrary inequality or cone
blocks would generally destroy their simple projections and is not this method.
The objective, primal variables and primal box are unchanged.

\section{Why the mixed-row metric has a factor of two}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:maj}
For the assumptions above,
\[
 M=\operatorname{diag}(2G,2\beta I)\succeq AA^\top,
 \qquad \norm{WA}_2^2\le2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exact decomposition is
\[
 M-AA^\top=
 \begin{bmatrix}E\\-C\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}E^\top&-C^\top\end{bmatrix}
 +\operatorname{diag}\bigl(0,2(\beta I-CC^\top)\bigr)\succeq0.
\]
Equivalently, the two row blocks of $WA$ each have squared operator norm at
most one. Their squared norms add when applied to any primal vector.
\end{proof}
Using $\operatorname{diag}(G,\beta I)$ instead would omit control of the cross
term $EC^\top$. Even $E=C=[1]$, $\beta=1$, gives a negative eigenvalue for
$\operatorname{diag}(G,\beta I)-AA^\top$. With equality rows only, $M=G$
would suffice; the implemented path retains the tested factor two for a common
rule. It does not silently change this coefficient on equality-only models.

\section{Pulled-back HPR row update}
Use the solver's dual sign convention $Qw+c-A^\top y-z=0$.
Let $v$ denote the primal argument presented to the row stage. In the existing
ordinary $w$--$y$--$w$ sweep it is
\[
 v=\widehat x+\sigma(Qw-Q\bar w_1).
\]
In whitened coordinates the usual scalar-majorized row stage uses the valid
bound $\widetilde\lambda_A=2$. Pulling that step back gives
\begin{align}
 Gq&=Ev-b, & \bar y_E&=y_E-\frac{q}{2\sigma},\label{eq:ystep}\\
 \tau&=2\sigma\beta, &
 \bar y_R&=\frac{\Pi_D(Cv-d-\tau y_R)-(Cv-d-\tau y_R)}{\tau}.
\end{align}
In implementation the second line uses the original row units; scaling and
unscaling its projection gives precisely this expression. The subsequent HPR
reflection, Halpern averaging, primal-box projection, and both objective sweeps
keep their existing forms. A general positive-semidefinite $Q$ is compatible
with the coordinate argument. The available conic implementation has narrower
eligibility than the argument; see Section~\ref{sec:policy}.

\subsection{Relation to a classical equality ADMM solve}
An unproximalized equality-coordinate ADMM minimization, with the other dual
block held fixed and residual argument $v$, has correction
\[
 G(\bar y_E-y_E)=-\frac{Ev-b}{\sigma}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:ystep} uses the same Gram linear solver but half that
correction. Equivalently it retains an equality proximal term with matrix
$\sigma G$, in addition to the equality augmented-Lagrangian curvature
$\sigma G$. It is therefore an ADMM-style Gram update with a conservative
proximal safeguard, not the literal undamped equality minimization in every
mixed-row problem. Removing the factor two requires a separately justified
majorizer or block-update scheme. Neither change is part of this branch.

\section{Transporting the algorithm, including its controllers}
The coordinate change does not require a new HPR splitting. It does require
consistent geometry wherever the scalar row majorizer appears. For an increment
$\Delta y$, the whitened squared norm is
\[
 2\norm{\Delta\widetilde y}^2
 =\Delta y^\top M\Delta y
 =2\norm{E^\top\Delta y_E}^2+2\beta\norm{\Delta y_R}^2.
\]
Consequently the implementation replaces each row term
$\lambda_A\norm{\Delta y}^2$ in its restart norm and penalty objective by this
quadratic. Cross terms through $A^\top\Delta y$ are invariant under
\eqref{eq:W}. The objective-side majorizer remains the existing one. It would
be inconsistent to change only the equality update while retaining Euclidean
row geometry in those controllers.

For the general-QP implementation the initial finite-bound scale is transported
as well. If $h_R$ denotes the finite-endpoint magnitude used by the source
initialization, its squared norm becomes
\[
 b^\top G^{-1}b+\norm{h_R}^2/\beta.
\]
The original finite-range guards and fallback initialization remain. The tested
legacy conic path has homogeneous equalities and preserves its previously
qualified scalar initialization. Thus coordinate equivalence of the core update
is distinguished from the particular initialization heuristic.

All stopping residuals are evaluated in the unscaled native units. A small
residual in whitened coordinates is not substituted for native feasibility.
The coordinate identities inherit the applicability of an HPR result under its
own assumptions; they do not establish a new convergence theorem for arbitrary
adaptive restart and penalty rules.

\section{Eligibility and failure policy}\label{sec:policy}
The two public controls are independent: polishing can be used without Gram,
and Gram without polishing. Gram is selected before iteration, not toggled
according to elapsed time or a later residual.
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{OFF}: preserve the ordinary path.
\item \texttt{AUTO}: conservatively recognize the tested spatial row-SOC
family from the actual matrix: homogeneous equality rows, a five-point tensor
Laplacian state block, positive diagonal state curvature and three-dimensional
row cones. No filename or benchmark ID is used. Detection is a performance
heuristic, not a guarantee for every member of the family.
\item \texttt{REQUEST}: attempt the implemented general-QP equality or legacy
conic path. The QP path permits affine equalities, general $Q$ and interleaved
interval rows. The conic path currently requires homogeneous equality prefixes,
diagonal $Q$, and no variable cones. Unsupported structures decline.
\end{itemize}
Numerical factorization and inverse checks must pass. No diagonal regularization
is inserted to disguise rank deficiency. Missing cuDSS, unsupported structure,
assembly/resource limits and rejected setup decline to ordinary HPR with setup
cost charged. A failure after the metric is active must propagate as an error;
it cannot silently change the HPR geometry in mid-solve. The fixed $G$ factor
is reused across penalty changes.

Broad Gram activation previously regressed full-corpus coverage. Its theoretical
eligibility is therefore not sufficient for automatic performance selection.
The conservative spatial rule reflects the selected co5\_1/cont5 evidence;
transfer to another matched model still requires measurement. The separate
exact spatial Gram-access acceleration is not included in this integration.

\section{Independent CPU polishing contract}
The GPU owner exports the immutable full unscaled native model and a consistent
copy of $(x,w,z,y)$. One isolated CPU process applies a simplified correction:
a QP active-bound linear solve, a frozen-factor sparse/banded residual
correction, or one structurally justified spatial correction. There is no
general iterative SSN refresh/line-search driver and no model-started independent
optimizer. All changes remain private to that process.

Admission uses native total error or the maximum of primal/dual equation errors
crossing successive decades, starting at $10^{-2}$ and ending at $10$ times
the requested tolerance. Initialization is skipped. While a worker runs,
crossings coalesce; after it finishes, a fresh eligible current HPR snapshot is
used. Wall time limits bound resource use but do not trigger admission. The
validated operational budgets are configurable, including disabling their
separate limits; they are not mathematical claims of unpolishability.

A separate CPU checker recomputes primal feasibility, dual stationarity/domain
feasibility and relative objective gap from the full native model and serialized
candidate. It uses actual interval support endpoints and requires a feasible
SOC dual domain. Its residual normalizations and objective-gap convention match
the native categories; additional finite-support and box checks are safeguards.
Acceptance requires all three categories to be finite and no larger than the
requested tolerance. A token and payload hash bind the certificate to this
solve and candidate. Failed or partial candidates never modify GPU iterates,
anchors, penalty or restart state. A passing candidate populates host result
buffers and terminates the solve; the GPU receives no candidate upload.
When HPR terminates first, its worker is cancelled and reaped.

\section{Evidence and scope}
The source provenance is recorded in \texttt{POLISHING\_SOURCE\_PROVENANCE.json}.
The mathematical Gram identities were qualified in S705; broad performance
regressions were measured in S704; the selective policy with simplified
polishing was evaluated in S707 (36/42 solved, six CPU acceptances). Those are
historical measurements across documented sessions, not timings of this newly
packaged branch. Branch validation checks source packaging, native certificates,
controller lifecycle, Gram coordinate identities and fallback behavior.
New runtime claims require complete process timings including setup, worker,
checking and cleanup costs, counting asynchronous overlap once.
